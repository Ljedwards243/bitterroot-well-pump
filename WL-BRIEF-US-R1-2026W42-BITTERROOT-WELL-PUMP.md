## 4. Verified local facts (use these; do not invent others)

**Wells and groundwater**
- The number of water wells in the Bitterroot Valley rose from about 3,100 in 1970 to 25,375 in 2023 (source: Montana Bureau of Mines and Geology hydrogeologist Sara Edinberg, presentation to Ravalli County Commissioners, reported in Bitterroot Star, "A look at groundwater in the Bitterroot," April 30, 2024).
- New well installations dipped during the 2008-2011 downturn but otherwise climbed steadily, mostly for domestic use; only about 4% of well use is for irrigation, since most valley irrigation uses surface water (same source).
- Domestic groundwater use in the valley rose from 2.14 to 2.48 million gallons per day between 2000 and 2015 (same source).
- Well water levels generally follow precipitation trends; MBMG hydrographs show the 2020-2023 drought in valley well levels. MBMG monitors 38 wells in Ravalli County for long-term water levels (same source). Use only as "water levels in valley wells rise and fall with wet and dry years; MBMG tracks them."
- Water quality in valley wells is generally good: nitrate almost all under the 10 mg/L drinking water standard and total dissolved solids mostly under 500 mg/L, with a cluster of wells in the foothills of the Willow Creek drainage showing high naturally occurring arsenic (same source). This is the ONLY location-specific water quality claim allowed.
- The Montana Bureau of Mines and Geology runs the Ground Water Information Center (GWIC), an online database with well logs for most wells in Montana. A homeowner's well log shows depth, water level, and the geology around the well (source: MSU Extension Well Educated "Find Your Well Log" guide; GWIC at mbmggwic.mtech.edu).

**Water testing (MSU Extension Well Educated program)**
- Testing a private well is the homeowner's responsibility; MSU Extension recommends testing every year for bacteria and nitrate (source: MSU Extension, "Testing Private Well Water Quality: The Owner's Responsibility").
- Statewide, the Well Educated program has found total coliform bacteria in about 25% of private well samples, and arsenic and manganese above health thresholds in about 10% of wells tested (source: MSU Extension). Statewide figures only; never present as Ravalli County percentages.
- Well Educated test kits are available at participating county offices; the program is not intended for real estate or legal purposes, so home buyers should use a Montana-certified lab directly (source: waterquality.montana.edu/well-ed/testing). Present as "check the program site for current Ravalli County kit availability."

**Licensing and registration (Montana)**
- It is unlawful for a water well contractor, water well driller, or monitoring well constructor to construct, drill, alter, abandon, decommission, or rehabilitate a water well without a license from the Board of Water Well Contractors, with an exception for owners working on their own farm, ranch, or residential land under a Board permit (source: MCA 37-43-302, Montana Code Annotated 2025).
- **Montana has no state license for well pump installers.** HB 707 (2007), which would have created pump installer and pump installation contractor licenses, was tabled in the House Business and Labor Committee and died (source: 2007 Montana Legislature bill record and committee minutes, Feb 22, 2007). Present as: "Montana licenses well drillers and well contractors, not pump installers, so ask about experience and registration instead."
- DNRC publishes a list of licensed drillers and contractors by company or location on the Board of Water Well Contractors page (source: dnrc.mt.gov, Well Contractors and Drillers).
- All construction contractors with employees must hold a Construction Contractor Registration with the Montana Department of Labor & Industry; DLI advises hiring parties to ask for proof and verify it on the DLI website or at (406) 444-7734 (source: erd.dli.mt.gov, Construction Contractor Registration).
- Montana requires a license to work as an electrical contractor or electrician (source: MCA 37-68-301). Use only as a question to ask ("Who does the electrical work?"), never as a statement that pump wiring legally requires an electrician.

**Cost benchmarks (general, not local quotes)**
- Angi's Hamilton, MT well and pump page lists an average well pump repair cost of about $950, with most repairs between $100 and $2,900 (source: Angi, Hamilton MT wells and pumps list page). Present as a general benchmark.
- This Old House (updated March 2026) puts typical well pump replacement at about $900 to $2,500 depending on pump type, well depth, and installation complexity (source: thisoldhouse.com well pump cost guide). National benchmark only.

**Do NOT state:** pump lifespans in years (no reliable source), average well depths in the valley, that any area other than the Willow Creek drainage foothills has a water quality problem, response times or "24/7" (unless a signed tenant offers it), install or repair durations, flat prices, that our pros are "licensed pump installers" (no such license exists), or any health effect of a contaminant beyond "above the health-based standard."
